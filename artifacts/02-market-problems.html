3. Problem detail

MP-02 — What changed between two dates

  • Persona: UP-PM (also UP-VDC)
  • Statement: "I can't see what changed on site between two dates without opening both captures and scanning every station."
  • Evidence:
    • WS-D2 — change report heat map prototype built from Atlas stations and frames; hackathon centred on it (D-14).
    • WS-D3 — hack agent built with deterministic tools and skills for heat-map reports.
    • WS-SH — presented to stakeholders; viewer change map made deterministic after the model hallucinated.
    • CUST-2HAND (WS-D2) — "Contractors confirmed the high practical value of the change heat map workflow."
  • Impact rationale: 4. Without it, the PM spends time scanning captures or sends someone to look; missed changes cost rework. Not yet shown to be a minimum purchase criterion (5).
  • Urgent: hypothesis — urgency tied to weekly coordination meetings; unproven.
  • Pervasive: hypothesis — every project with two or more captures has the question; frequency of asking unknown.
  • Willing to pay: hypothesis — contractor reaction positive; nobody asked about price.
  • Validation: VB-01

MP-01 — Which open items can be closed

  • Persona: UP-PM (also UP-SUPER)
  • Statement: "I can't tell which open RFIs, punch items and BCF topics could be closed without sending someone to check each one."
  • Evidence:
    • WS-D1 — "they're going to spend the next five hours sifting through spec to find it … then compare it to the photo"; OpenSpace demo resolving an RFI and closing a punch item agent-to-agent.
    • WS-D2 — closable-items workflow specified end to end: aggregate, classify, evidence report, accept or reject.
    • WS-PW — named the strongest candidate for the first workflow slice.
  • Impact rationale: 4. Open items hold up closeout and retention release; checking them is repeated site time.
  • Urgent: hypothesis — likely peaks near handover; unproven.
  • Pervasive: hypothesis — every ProjectSight project has RFIs and punch items; share with reality-verifiable items unknown.
  • Willing to pay: hypothesis — competitor investment (OpenSpace) suggests value; no customer price signal.
  • Validation: VB-02

MP-03 — Completeness against schedule and design

  • Persona: UP-PM (also UP-SUPER)
  • Statement: "I don't know how complete an area is against schedule and design until a daily report arrives, and those are late and inaccurate."
  • Evidence:
    • WS-D1 — customers "struggle with manual reporting delays" (stated by staff, not relayed from a named customer); 77% of projects deliver at least 40% late (IND).
    • WS-D2 — progress-to-plan heat map workflow; hackathon focus (D-14); daily reports late and inaccurate.
    • WS-SH — progress-to-plan presented as the second hackathon output.
  • Impact rationale: 4. Late visibility of slippage costs time and money.
  • Urgent / Pervasive / Willing to pay: hypothesis — OpenSpace sells progress tracking with professional services (WS-D2), which suggests willingness to pay in the market, but not for us.
  • Dependencies: schedule data (APE) and design alignment, both unresolved (see §4).
  • Validation: VB-03

MP-04 — Evidence for payment

  • Persona: UP-QS (buyer BP-SUB)
  • Statement: "I float labour and materials until I can prove what was installed, and assembling that proof delays payment."
  • Evidence:
    • WS-D1 — payment evidence discussed as a subcontractor driver.
    • WS-D2 — "Subcontractors operate as cash-flow negative businesses and require reality capture evidence for rapid payment"; "that's a global problem".
    • V1-PRD — OpenSpace Phase 2 integration: visual payment validation deep links from the Trimble Pay schedule of values.
  • Impact rationale: 4. Direct cash-flow cost to the subcontractor.
  • Urgent / Pervasive / Willing to pay: hypothesis — strongest stated willingness-to-pay story in the workshop; no subcontractor has been asked.
  • Validation: VB-04

MP-13 — Trust in AI answers (cross-cutting)

  • Persona: all users; written for UP-PM
  • Statement: "I can't trust an AI answer I can't check, and a wall of text doesn't let me check it."
  • Evidence:
    • WS-D2 — trust-but-verify; deterministic tools for facts.
    • WS-D3 — Assist outputs walls of text with emojis.
    • WS-SH — the model hallucinated heat maps; the change map was made deterministic.
    • CUST-2HAND (WS-D1) — "a more visual output … is what our customers are wanting."
  • Impact rationale: 3. Untrusted answers stop use of every workflow; does not block a goal on its own.
  • Treatment: not a group. Drives NFRs and the P-TRUST enabler, and acceptance criteria in every story.
  • Validation: VB-05

  • Persona: UP-FIELD

  • Impact rationale: 3. Missing coverage makes other workflows unanswerable; a change comparison needs both dates covered.
  • Grouping: with MP-02 because a change answer is only as good as the coverage of both captures.
  • Three-part test: hypothesis on all legs.
  • Validation: VB-06

MP-07 — Re-walking to verify fixes

  • Persona: UP-SUPER · Evidence: WS-D2 · Impact: 3 · Test: hypothesis · Validation: VB-12

In ProjectSight, verification usually happens when an item sits in a reinspection or pending approval status after the trade reports work complete — not only when the record is already Closed. The pain is reviewing that queue without walking every location; push-back to Open when a fix does not hold up is a normal ProjectSight action (WF-06 link-out), not something Maya performs.
