Competitive Landscape — reality-capture intelligence for contractors

Who else solves this, and where are the gaps? · Owner: product definer

Company: Trimble · Product: Maya (MAP) · Author: product definer (generated draft) · Date: 2026-09-28

Comparison universe: OpenSpace (competitor and integration partner), Dalux, DroneDeploy, FARO Sphere, Autodesk Construction Cloud, Disperse; plus the alternatives customers actually use today — walking the site, daily reports and spreadsheets, professional-services progress tracking, a general-purpose AI assistant with connectors, in-house builds, and doing nothing.

Read-out. The two highest-scoring groups are also the cheapest, because they reuse data Trimble already holds (WS-D1 D-03). Competitors have concentrated on high-investment, high-accuracy progress tracking (WS-D2: "everybody in the market … has gone to the high end"). The open space is good-enough answers tied to the project record.

3. Where we win

  • Records plus reality. Closing RFIs, punch items and BCF topics needs both the record and the capture. Trimble holds both, with history (MP-01). The "only Trimble" part is unproven until the ProjectSight and Connect data can be reached through P-ACCESS.
  • Change over time. A full timeline of captures on one project, not a point-in-time scan (MP-02) (WS-D1).
  • Inside the product the customer already uses. v1 adoption came from fitting into ProjectSight (WS-D1); MAP is embedded, not a new portal (MP-09).
  • Trust through restraint. Declining with a path to yes, against competitors' confident answers (MP-13). assumption that customers reward this; to be tested.

4. Where we should not fight

  • Millimetre-accurate automated progress against BIM — competitors and services have years of lead and it is high investment.
  • Sensor hardware and point-cloud fusion.
  • Design authoring.
  • General-purpose chat.
  • Open: OpenSpace and Disperse are both competitors and integration partners in V1-PRD. Whether MAP answers questions over their captures is a strategy decision, not an engineering one. > TODO: owner product leadership.
